Wren · Pico programmer wiring

A Waveshare RP2040 Plus (or any Pico) as a Thing's programmer, shown on the OLED Thing. Seen from above, USB at the top.

Parts

  • Waveshare RP2040 Plus (or any Pico-pinout RP2040 board), straddling the breadboard's gap: pins in columns c and h, rows 1–20, USB at the top.
  • One 470 Ω resistor: yellow, violet, brown, gold. The Thing has about 1.2 nF on SWIM, so it needs a strong pull-up. Not below about 410 Ω: the Thing's pin sinks 8 mA at most.
  • Four jumper wires to the Thing, two short ones on the breadboard.

3.3 V only

  • The Thing runs from the board's 3V3(OUT). Nothing else may power it while the board is connected: RP2040 pins are not 5 V tolerant.
  • Wire everything with USB unplugged, then check each wire against the table.

The firmware

  1. Hold BOOTSEL, plug in USB, let go: a drive called RPI-RP2 appears.
  2. Drag firmware/PicoLink/PicoLink.uf2 onto it (Wren's own), or firmware/pico-swimlib/pico-espstlink.uf2. The board restarts as a USB serial port; Wren finds either.
  3. Both time SWIM in hardware with the RP2040's PIO, using Richard Hulme's pico-swimlib (BSD 3-Clause).
